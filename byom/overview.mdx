BYOM (Bring Your Own Model) runs every job IronBee runs for your account on your model instead of IronBee's:

- [code reviews](/code-review/overview), including the ones `ironbee review` starts from the CLI
- the [code wiki](/console/code-wiki)
- [code security](/code-security/overview) scans
- platform [verification runs](/console/verifications)

The jobs still run on IronBee's infrastructure, as before. Only their model calls change: IronBee's model proxy makes every call to your endpoint, an HTTPS address you give, in Anthropic Messages, OpenAI Chat Completions or OpenAI Responses format. Your endpoint can be:

- your own Anthropic API key
- your own OpenAI API key
- a server or gateway of yours that speaks one of those APIs, such as vLLM, SGLang or Ollama

The CLI's own verification cycles run in your AI coding agent, on its model, so BYOM doesn't change them.

## Who sets it up, and where

BYOM is managed in the console alone, under **Settings > BYOM**. No API route or CLI command manages it. See [BYOM settings](/console/byom).

- **Owners and admins** set up BYOM and change its settings.
- **Every other role** sees the page read-only: whether BYOM is on, its format, its host, its model and the names of its headers. Never the key or a header's value.
- **Setting up BYOM needs the Enterprise plan.** BYOM settings made earlier keep working whatever the plan becomes, and editing, enabling, disabling and deleting them need no plan. See [Pricing](/console/pricing).
- **One model for the whole account.** It serves every call of every job. A model per team, per project, per product or per task isn't offered.

## Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Prepare your endpoint">
    For your own server, allow IronBee's addresses and require the key on every path. For a provider's API, make a key for BYOM alone. See [Prepare your endpoint](/byom/your-endpoint).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the form">
    Open **Settings > BYOM** and pick **Anthropic**, **OpenAI** or **Other**. A preset fills in what its provider fixes, so you give the key and the model. **Other** asks for the format, the base URL and how the key is sent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save** or **Save and enable**. Saving runs a connection test first, which takes up to 90 seconds, and shows its report. See [Save and the connection test](/console/byom#save-and-the-connection-test).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable">
    Once the test passes, click **Enable**. From the next job, every job of the account runs on your model. Jobs already running finish on IronBee's model.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The three formats

Your endpoint speaks one format, the one you pick or your provider's preset fixes. A server may serve more than one, so IronBee doesn't guess it.

| Format | IronBee calls | Typical endpoints |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Anthropic Messages** | `<base URL>/v1/messages` | Anthropic's API (the **Anthropic** preset), vLLM, SGLang, Ollama |
| **OpenAI Chat Completions** | `<base URL>/chat/completions` | vLLM, SGLang, Ollama, a hosted gateway such as OpenRouter |
| **OpenAI Responses** | `<base URL>/responses` | OpenAI's API (the **OpenAI** preset), and servers that work as it does |

Which one suits your server, and what each one changes for verification and code review, is on [Formats and models](/byom/formats-and-models).

## Credits and your provider's bill

On BYOM two bills meet:

- **Your provider or your server** pays for every model call, at its own rates. The console doesn't show your model's token usage: your provider's or your server's records hold it.
- **IronBee** still charges credits for the jobs that use them. A job on BYOM is charged the rate card's credits times 0.5, rounded up to a whole credit. A verification's started minutes are halved the same way. What's free stays free, and the code wiki uses no credits either way.

While BYOM is on, the rate card on [Credit usage](/console/usage#what-a-job-costs) says so, the prices on the code review and code security settings pages are already halved, and each charge made on your model is marked **BYOM**. Each plan's credits are on [Pricing](/console/pricing).

Jobs no person starts also run on your model, and your provider bills them: a repository's scheduled wiki refreshes, scheduled code security scans, and the [follow-up runs](/code-security/how-a-scan-works#follow-up-runs) after a scan. Follow-up runs are free on IronBee's side and billed in full by your provider. Measured on 2026-11-22 on `gpt-5.4` at OpenAI's API, a scan and its two follow-up runs together cost about 2.5 times the scan alone at the provider.

<Warning>
Set a spending or a rate limit on your gateway or at your provider. IronBee's caps on a job's tokens guard against a job that runs away, and aren't a budget. On BYOM, code security runs with no spending stop of IronBee's.
</Warning>

## What reaches your endpoint

From every job claimed while BYOM is enabled:

- the code the job reads: diffs, files, the repository's contents a tool returns, the wiki's facts
- a pull request's or merge request's text, its comments, and the replies the reviewer reads
- a verification's target pages and API answers, and the names of its run's secrets, never their values
- IronBee's prompts: the reviewer's, the code wiki's and code security's
- on verification, Claude Code's own texts in its requests: its identity sentence, an environment entry, the definitions of its built-in tools (Edit, Glob, Grep, Read, WebFetch, Write), and in longer sessions its reminders and its compaction prompt

No account id, job id or user is sent. Nothing reaches a third party for the model: IronBee's own model isn't called for a job claimed while BYOM is enabled. What reaches your repository's host and your deployment's provider is the same as on IronBee's model.

In OpenAI Responses format every call is sent with `store: false`, so IronBee asks the endpoint to keep no response of a job. Your model's reasoning comes back as content the endpoint encrypted, rides inside the job's conversation and goes back to the same endpoint; neither IronBee nor the job's machine can read it. What your provider itself keeps of a request is its own policy and your agreement with it.

What stays on IronBee's side: the code, inside IronBee's machines while a job runs; the job's records and the wiki; the findings, the jobs and their usage counts; and IronBee's logs, which hold no request or answer. IronBee runs its jobs, and its calls to your endpoint, in the United States (AWS us-west-2, Oregon).

BYOM moves the model, not the code. To keep the code inside your network, use the self-hosted runner.

## BYOM and the self-hosted runner

BYOM and the [self-hosted runner](/code-review/self-hosted-runner/overview) both run on a model of yours, in different places:

| | BYOM | Self-hosted runner |
|---|---|---|
| **What runs on your model** | Every job: code review, the code wiki, code security, verification | Code review only |
| **Where the jobs run** | IronBee's cloud | Your CI: GitHub Actions, GitLab CI/CD or Bitbucket Pipelines, from IronBee's image |
| **Where your code is read** | IronBee's machines | Your CI job |
| **Who calls your model** | IronBee's model proxy, from IronBee's addresses | Your CI job |
| **A model on a private network** | Not supported: the endpoint is public HTTPS | Supported |
| **What it needs** | The Enterprise plan | An Enterprise agreement and a license |
| **Credits** | Charged at half the rate card | None: the license covers it |
| **Results** | As in IronBee's cloud: findings, wiki pages and records in the console | The review on the pull request; the console shows counts |
| **Set up in** | **Settings > BYOM** | Your CI, with `ironbee runner` |

BYOM doesn't change the runner: the runner calls the model its own settings name.

## What isn't supported

- A model on a private network, reached through a private link or a relay. The self-hosted runner reviews code with such a model.
- An endpoint that answers without a key.
- A cloud's model service reached with the cloud's own identity (an assumed role, a service account, a workload identity) instead of a key.
- A query parameter on the base URL, such as Azure's `api-version`.
- A subscription's sign-in, such as a Claude or ChatGPT plan, in place of an API key.
- A second model, or a model per team, per project, per product or per task.
- Client certificates (mutual TLS), and OAuth or Entra tokens that IronBee would refresh.
- A fallback model: while BYOM is enabled, no job moves to IronBee's model, and none to another model of yours.
- A spending budget set for a run. Set one on your gateway or at your provider.
- Managing BYOM through the public API or the CLI.
- A summary of your model's reasoning shown to anyone: none is asked of the Responses API.
- A chat template whose rules go beyond one tool round trip, such as nine-character tool-call ids, or no user message right after tool messages. The test's tool step catches a template that refuses IronBee's second turn.
- Images and documents given by URL, and documents that aren't text on OpenAI Chat Completions and OpenAI Responses. A short note takes their place.
- Your model's token usage in the console.
